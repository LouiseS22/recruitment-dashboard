Stage-by-Stage Guide
Recruiter Screening
Run by: Recruiter

Goal

  • Qualify interest, logistics, and baseline fit before consuming manager time

Activities

  • Confirm right to work
  • Walk through the role and company
  • Gauge motivation and salary expectations
  • Assess communication and enthusiasm
What good looks like: Candidate has done their research, is clear on why Deliverance, and has realistic expectations.
First Stage
Run by: Hiring Manager

Goal

  • Assess competency, role fit, and culture alignment

Activities

  • Role overview and context (10 min)
  • 2–3 STAR questions relevant to the role
  • Candidate Q&A
  • Close with clear next steps
What good looks like: Specific evidence-based answers, genuine curiosity about the product and mission, coachable and self-aware.
Second Stage
Run by: Panel or includes a task

Goal

  • Go deeper on skills, assess how they think and work

Activities

  • Technical or skills-based task or case study
  • Second round of STAR questions — different competencies
  • Informal meet with a team member
What good looks like: Structured thinking under pressure, collaborative approach, intellectual honesty about gaps.
Final Stage
Run by: Senior Leadership

Goal

  • Values alignment, mutual fit, and readiness to move to offer

Activities

  • Vision and culture conversation
  • Discussion of growth and career trajectory at Deliverance
  • Candidate's questions about the Series A, roadmap, and team
What good looks like: Genuine excitement about the scale-up moment, alignment with our values, asking smart questions.
STAR Framework
The STAR framework is used across First and Second Stage interviews. It helps interviewers gather structured, evidence-based answers rather than opinions or hypotheticals. Always push for a real past example — redirect with "Tell me about a time when…" if the candidate goes hypothetical.
S
Situation
Set the scene — what was the context, when did this happen, and what were the stakes?
Follow-up: "Tell me more about the context. How large was the team / organisation / project at the time?"
T
Task
Define the challenge — what specifically were you personally responsible for?
Follow-up: "What was your personal accountability here? What would have happened if you hadn't stepped in?"
A
Action
Show the work — what did you do, how, and why those choices over alternatives?
Follow-up: "Walk me through your decision-making. What alternatives did you consider and why did you choose this approach?"
R
Result
Prove the impact — what changed? Ideally with numbers or measurable outcomes.
Follow-up: "What was the measurable outcome? What would you do differently in hindsight?"

Watch-outs

  • Candidate says "we" throughout — push for individual contribution with "What was your specific role in that?"
  • Hypothetical answers — redirect with "That's useful context, but can you give me a real example when you did this?"
  • Results with no numbers or evidence of measurable impact — probe harder
  • Rehearsed stories that seem to fit every question — vary your probes to test depth

Dos & Don'ts
During Any Interview

Do

  • Set the agenda clearly at the start
  • Take notes throughout the interview
  • Use follow-up probes after STAR answers
  • Give the candidate time for their Q&A
  • Sell the company honestly, including the Series A stage
  • Ask the same core questions across all candidates for that role

Don't

  • Ask hypothetical "what would you do" questions
  • Lead the witness or signal the answer you want
  • Fill silences before the candidate has finished thinking
  • Spend too long pitching and rush the evaluation
  • Compare candidates or mention other applicants
  • Make commitments on offer, start date, or equity
Legal & Compliance

Do

  • Ask job-relevant questions only
  • Take factual, objective notes
  • Conduct a right to work check at the appropriate stage
  • Apply an equal, consistent process for all candidates

Don't

  • Ask about age, nationality, ethnicity, religion, or political views
  • Ask about family plans or pregnancy
  • Ask about health or disability unless a genuine occupational requirement
  • Record notes on appearance, accent, or personal characteristics
After Each Stage

Post-Interview Actions

  • Submit your scorecard within 24 hours of the interview
  • Share your view before the debrief to reduce groupthink
  • All decisions must reference interview evidence, not gut feel
  • The recruiter coordinates all candidate communication — do not contact candidates directly
